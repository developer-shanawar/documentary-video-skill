# Engaging Scripting — Documentary Video Skill

Engaging does not mean loud. It means the viewer always knows what question we are answering, and always gets a new visual or a new fact every 10 seconds.

## 1. Hook in the first 15 seconds

Pick one formula, then a question. Do not shout.

- Counter-claim + question: "Sab kehte hain [common belief]. Lekin [evidence] kuch aur kehta hai — to phir asal me kya hua?"
- One number + question: "[Number] saal pehle, [place] me [surprising fact]. Ye kaise mumkin tha?"
- A question the viewer already has: "Agar [place] itna advanced tha, to wo gaya kahan?"

Show the biggest visual first. Do not start with a long introduction.

## 2. One question per part

Each 1-minute part answers one question, and ends on a new question. That is the open loop that keeps the viewer watching.

- Part 1: What is the mystery?
- Part 2: How advanced was it, really?
- Part 3: What clues do we have?
- Part 4: What do theories say — as theories?
- Part 5: What most likely happened, and what remains today?

Never answer everything in part 1. Never repeat the same question in every part.

## 3. Pattern interrupt every beat

Every 10-second beat must change something: a new visual, a new number, a new camera angle, a cutaway, a diagram, a real image.

- Same visual for two beats in a row = boring. Replace it.
- A list of facts with no question, no number, and no visual change = boring. Add a question or a number in 3 pictures.
- A beat where the voice says one thing and the screen shows another = confusing. Fix the visual, not the voice.

## 4. Numbers that stick

Use one number, and show it in 3 pictures: as a diagram, as a map, and as a real image. Same value each time.

Example: a length, a depth, a population described as "lakhon log" when the exact number is uncertain — never invent a fixed number to sound precise.

## 5. Evidence before opinion

Science / evidence first, then an explicit pivot to text or interpretation, if any.

- Fact: what was found, where, when.
- Disputed: what experts disagree on.
- Interpretation: what someone thinks it means — labelled interpretation, never presented as proof.

## 6. Payoff and ending

Every video ends with a payoff and a one-line lesson, then an open question + personal sign-off.

- Payoff: what we now know that we did not know at the start.
- Lesson: one line, in plain words.
- Ending: "[Open question]? Aap kya sochte hain — comment me batayein."

No hard subscribe graphic. No "agle part mein samjhenge" promise at the end of every part.

## 7. Voice lines that work

- Short sentences. One idea per line. About 25–30 Roman Urdu words per 10-second beat.
- Say the timeline year out loud when it appears on screen.
- No stage directions inside the spoken lines. No "dekhiye" repeated in every beat.
